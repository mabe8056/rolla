## Job G (heavy, top priority right after #38 lands): ROLLA must actually *know the genre*

### Melak's vision (his words, paraphrased)
The reference tracks, transcriptions and genre research were **training material** for understanding UK minimal / tech house, not templates to copy. ROLLA should absorb that understanding and use it to make its **own original choices** from Melak's library:
- **Sound selection and curation**: what a good kit in this genre is, and which sounds mesh together.
- **Mix**: how these genres are balanced (levels, low end, brightness, space per lane).
- **Rhythm and groove**: drums, swing, ghost notes, percussion interplay.
- **Songwriting**: basslines, stab parts, call and response, harmony, how a loop develops and turns around (bar-4 and bar-8 turnarounds, variations).

No note-for-note copying, no per-track imitation, and no reference note data or artist names in the public repo.

### Step 1: audit (post on this issue first, short)
For each of the four areas, state where genre knowledge is encoded in code today (file and function), whether it's a general rule or tendency, a hand-written per-track imitation, or missing, and how picks happen now (random, weighted, seeded). Sources to fold in: GENRE_KNOWLEDGE.md, PATTERN_VOCAB.json, GENERATOR_RULES.md, the transcriptions and briefs, the corpus-driven generator, and the mix-strip targets.

### Step 2: build the genre brain (general rules and tendencies, per style ROLLER / DEEP / BOUNCY / JACKIN)
1. **Kit curation and pairing**
   - Analyse every sample in the pack at build or index time: spectral centroid and brightness, attack and decay length, low-end energy, detected root pitch and key for tonal files, loudness, noisiness.
   - Choose a kit as a set, not lane by lane at random. Score candidate kits on genre pairing rules:
     - The kick and the bass share the low end without masking each other.
     - Tonal parts (bass, stabs, pads, vox) are in the same key, or repitched to the roll's key.
     - Brightness and decay fit the style (DEEP darker and longer, BOUNCY punchier and brighter, and so on).
     - Hats and percussion have matching textures.
     - Avoid clashing pairs.
   - Prefer coherent sources (the same pack or family) as a tendency, not a rule.
   - Each roll picks a good kit that is different from the last one.
2. **Mix balance per style**: target relative lane levels, low-end ownership (kick vs bass, sidechain depth), per-lane brightness and space, expressed as statistics or tendencies. Feed them into the mix strip and gain staging (#36).
3. **Groove**: per-style swing amounts, ghost-note probabilities, hat and percussion interplay and density curves, as tendencies, never fixed patterns.
4. **Songwriting**: bass and stab phrasing grammar (call and response, rests, motif then variation), harmony choices, how a 4-, 8- or 16-bar loop develops, and turnarounds at bar 4 and bar 8. These are generated from rules and probabilities so each roll is original.

## Amendment to Key + Scale job: retune only if the sound survives (Melak-approved)

Retuning must never destroy a sound. If pitching a sample changes how it reads in the mix, **don't use that sound for the locked key; pick a different one that fits.**

**Rule:** before using a retuned sample, render the shifted version offline and compare it to the original. Reject it (skip the sample, re-pick from the pool) if any check fails.
- **Shift size:** prefer the smallest interval (pick the nearest in-scale root, up or down). Tonal and sustained sounds (pads, chords, vox) are capped at about ±3 semitones. Short stabs and plucks are capped at about ±5. Bass one-shots are capped at about ±4, and their fundamental must stay inside the style's sub band from the mix spec.
- **Tonality and spectrum:** the spectral centroid and tilt shouldn't move beyond tolerance, and the band balance has to still fit the lane's slot in the mix spec. A pad must not turn thin or honky, and a bass must not lose its sub or pile up low mids.
- **Waveform character:** transient sharpness and attack time, crest factor, and the harmonic-to-noise balance should stay close to the original. Reject zipper, graininess, smearing, or aliasing from the stretch, and any formant shift that makes a vocal sound chipmunked or slowed down.
- **Low end:** a retuned bass must stay mono-safe and not clash with the kick fundamental.
- **Tolerances:** derive them from the mix spec and reference data, and add them to config so they can be tuned without code changes.

**Behavior:** if no sample in a lane's pool passes for the locked key, fall back to the nearest workable sound and log it. Never ship a mangled one. In Auto mode, bias choices toward samples already in key (no shift).

**Acceptance (added):** the unit test asserts every retuned sample in 200 seeds per style passes the checks above, and there's a test showing an over-shifted pad gets rejected and replaced. The scorer gets a RETUNE_ARTIFACT check, which should be 0 on locked renders.
